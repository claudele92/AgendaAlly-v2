============================================================
2. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE
============================================================

Establish one authoritative, immutable financial answer for each payment:

WHO WAS SUPPOSED TO COLLECT THIS PAYMENT?

WHO ACTUALLY COLLECTED / IS TREATED AS COLLECTING IT?

WHAT COLLECTION MODEL APPLIED AT PAYMENT TIME?

WHAT PRINCIPAL, COMMISSION, VENDOR ENTITLEMENT AND PLATFORM LIABILITY FOLLOW
FROM THAT MODEL?

The collection decision used for financial accounting must not silently change
later because:

- Shop preferences change;
- Vendor payment settings change;
- provider settings change;
- provider availability changes;
- global activation changes;
- environment flags change;
- country/currency policy changes;
- Product/Service configuration changes;
- the Vendor later switches between AgendaAlly Payments and their own gateway.

Historical financial meaning must come from the persisted transaction/payment
context, not from today's Shop configuration.

============================================================

============================================================
9. PLATFORM-MANAGED ACCOUNTING
============================================================

For platform-managed electronic collection, establish the required accounting
invariant.

Conceptually:

Customer gross payment = G

AgendaAlly/platform actually collects G through platform merchant credentials.

Commission/platform revenue = C

Vendor economic entitlement = V

where, subject to the native fee model:

G = C + V + any explicitly modeled adjustments.

Do not invent a new fee formula.

Use the existing AgendaAlly fee/commission rules.

Determine exactly what record should represent:

- gross collected;
- platform commission;
- Vendor entitlement;
- amount payable to Vendor;
- amount already paid;
- refund/reversal;
- remaining liability.

The platform must not treat its own gross collection as pure revenue.

============================================================
10. VENDOR-DIRECT ACCOUNTING
============================================================

For Vendor-direct electronic collection, establish the opposite custody rule.

If Customer principal G is collected through the Vendor's merchant account:

AgendaAlly must NOT also create a platform liability to pay G to the Vendor.

Otherwise the Vendor could receive:

G from provider

AND

G again from AgendaAlly payout.

Determine what AgendaAlly should record under the existing intended model:

- authoritative gross transaction;
- Vendor-direct collection evidence;
- platform commission C;
- commission receivable/recovery state;
- any legitimate adjustment.

Do NOT implement a new commission collection mechanism yet unless the current
architecture already has one and a minimal correction is necessary for
accounting correctness.

If Vendor-direct commission recovery is incomplete, preserve that as an
explicit liability/receivable gap rather than fabricating settlement.

============================================================
11. NO FALSE PLATFORM PAYABLE
============================================================

This is a mandatory invariant:

VENDOR-DIRECT COLLECTION MUST NOT CREATE A PLATFORM PAYOUT OBLIGATION FOR
PRINCIPAL THE VENDOR ALREADY COLLECTED DIRECTLY.

Trace every path that creates:

- Vendor balance;
- Vendor payable;
- payout eligibility;
- payout requestable amount;
- platform fee ledger;
- Wallet credit associated with Vendor settlement.

Ensure collection context controls those decisions.

Do not rely solely on current Shop preference.

============================================================
12. NO MISSING PLATFORM PAYABLE
============================================================

The inverse invariant also applies.

If AgendaAlly/platform collected Customer principal on behalf of a Vendor,
the architecture must not lose the Vendor economic entitlement merely because:

- Shop preference later changes;
- provider configuration changes;
- payment method becomes unavailable;
- Vendor switches to Vendor-direct later.

The immutable collection context must preserve the platform's liability for the
specific transaction.
