B6. Evaluate the desired Vendor-owned driver model
==================================================

The preferred AgendaAlly direction is:

A Vendor should be able to manage its own Delivery Drivers from the Vendor portal.

A Vendor receives an order belonging to its business and can assign that order to one of its own active Delivery Drivers.

The driver later signs into the AgendaAlly Delivery app and sees only deliveries they are authorized to handle.

Evaluate whether this fits the existing architecture.

Prefer extending/reusing existing models and APIs rather than creating a competing delivery subsystem.

==================================================
B7. Evaluate registration vs invitation
==================================================

Determine the safest and cleanest onboarding model.

Preferred concept:

Vendor Portal
→ Delivery Drivers
→ Invite Delivery Driver

Vendor enters something such as:

- name;
- email and/or phone;
- optional employee/reference information.

AgendaAlly creates an invitation.

Driver receives invitation.

Driver:
→ opens invite
→ creates/signs into AgendaAlly account
→ accepts Vendor relationship
→ obtains Delivery Driver access for that business.

The Vendor should NOT need to know or create the driver's password.

Compare this with whatever the existing system currently does.

Recommend whether:

A. Vendor-created accounts;
B. invitation + driver self-registration;
C. existing AgendaAlly user linked as driver;
D. hybrid invitation supporting both new and existing users

best fits the current code.

Unless existing architecture strongly argues otherwise, prefer the hybrid invitation model:

Existing AgendaAlly user
→ accepts driver invitation.

New user
→ registers
→ accepts invitation.

==================================================
B8. Tenant isolation requirements
==================================================

The target design must enforce server-side:

Vendor A can manage:
- Vendor A's drivers;
- Vendor A's eligible orders.

Vendor A cannot:
- view Vendor B's private driver records;
- edit Vendor B's drivers;
- disable Vendor B's drivers;
- assign Vendor B's drivers;
- assign Vendor B's orders.

Driver A can access:
- Driver A's authorized profile;
- Driver A's assigned deliveries;
- only information required to perform those deliveries.

Driver A cannot:
- enumerate another driver's assignments;
- access another Vendor's unrelated orders;
- modify an order not assigned to them.

Staff access must follow existing Vendor staff/permission architecture.

Admin/Superadmin may retain broader access where intentionally authorized.

Do not rely on frontend filtering.

B11. Privacy and security
==================================================

Delivery drivers may need customer information to fulfill an order.

Audit what customer information is currently exposed to delivery actors.

Recommend least-privilege access.

A driver should receive only fulfillment-required information, such as where legitimately needed:

- customer display/name;
- delivery address;
- delivery instructions;
- appropriate contact mechanism;
- order/package information necessary for delivery.

Do not expose unrelated:

- customer account data;
- payment credentials;
- payment-provider details;
- internal financial records;
- Vendor secrets.

Determine whether access should stop or become restricted after delivery completion.
