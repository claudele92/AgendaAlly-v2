1. P0-A — unauthorized Customer first fulfillment settlement

The authenticated Customer Order-status endpoint can take a "new" Product Order to "delivered" and reach the shared fulfillment financial settlement service without sufficient ownership/settlement-authority enforcement.

Current source chain from the accepted stop report:

- "POST /api/v1/dashboard/user/orders/{id}/status/change"
- authenticated by "sanctum.check";
- "StatusUpdateRequest" accepts all native "Order::STATUSES", including "delivered";
- User "OrderController" performs an unscoped "Order::find($id)"-style lookup rather than binding the target to the authenticated Customer;
- its lifecycle check requires current "status=new" but does not restrict the requested transition to a Customer-authorized operation;
- it delegates to "OrderStatusUpdateService::statusUpdate";
- entering "delivered" invokes fulfillment financial effects including the gross Admin Wallet top-up and, for Cash, paid-state mutation.

This is a first-settlement authority defect.

A once-only settlement claim alone does not contain it because the unauthorized Customer could acquire the first claim.

---

2. P0-B — fulfillment financial replay

The original source-confirmed Product fulfillment replay remains uncontained.

Each entry into "delivered" can invoke fulfillment financial effects again.

A lifecycle sequence such as:

"delivered → another permitted non-canceled state → delivered"

can repeat the gross Admin Wallet credit without another Customer payment.

This is a financial-finality defect.

Authorization alone does not contain it because a legitimately authorized Seller or other fulfillment actor could still replay settlement.

---

3. Required security/accounting invariant

Treat these as two independent controls.

The resulting implementation must guarantee:

«A fulfillment-triggered financial settlement may be initiated only by an actor with native authority to perform the settlement-triggering lifecycle transition, and the financial effect for a given Order may commit at most once.»

Therefore:

authorization ≠ finality

and:

finality ≠ authorization

Both must be enforced server-side.

Do not rely on the frontend.

Do not rely on hidden buttons.

Do not rely on sequential request assumptions.

Do not treat a durable settlement marker as authorization.

Do not treat an authorization check as financial idempotency.

5. Customer route containment

Determine the intended native purpose of:

"POST /api/v1/dashboard/user/orders/{id}/status/change"

Preserve legitimate Customer functionality.

Do not simply add:

"where(user_id = authenticated customer)"

and consider the P0 contained.

Ownership and settlement authority are separate.

Even the owner of their own Order must not be able to declare fulfillment complete if native business policy reserves that operation for Seller/Admin/delivery actors.

The Customer path must therefore enforce both:

Target ownership

A Customer must not mutate another Customer's Order through this route.

Permitted Customer transition

The Customer may perform only the lifecycle operations that native business policy actually assigns to Customers.

If source establishes that this route exists only for Customer cancellation or another restricted operation, enforce that explicit transition contract.

Do not invent new Customer lifecycle powers.

Do not weaken existing cancellation/refund authorization.

9. Settlement atomicity

If containment is possible without a schema change, the once-only claim and required SQL financial effects must participate in the appropriate database transaction.

Prevent:

Sequential replay

"delivered → other permitted state → delivered"

must not create another fulfillment financial settlement.

Direct duplicate

Repeated "delivered" requests must not duplicate financial effects.

Stale-model replay

Two stale Order/service instances must not both settle.

Concurrent replay

Two concurrent requests must not both acquire settlement.

Do not use an application-only:

"if (!exists) { create(); }"

pattern without an actual atomic claim/locking/uniqueness boundary.

10. Failure atomicity

Explicitly handle both exceptions and native false-return financial operations.

If a required financial operation fails:

- do not retain a false settlement claim;
- do not retain only part of the required financial effects;
- do not mark the Order financially settled when settlement failed;
- permit a legitimate retry after rollback;
- do not silently swallow required financial failures.

Do not broaden this into redesigning every legacy financial service.
